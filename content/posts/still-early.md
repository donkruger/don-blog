I recently realised I have friends and family who don’t really know all that much about my working life.

I find myself conveying bits and pieces, usually in passing. mainly explaining what my title as Head of Platform is all about and all the remark-worthy shenanigans we’ve been up to.

I think this blog is also an opportunity to share what a year it has been in my professional life through the lens of what I hope are a few relatable reflections, and shining some light on the achievements that our team has worked hard on in 2026.

Five years in, I feel I have worn the badge long enough to write about that more personally.

(I am not speaking for EasyEquities here. I am speaking as someone who has had the privilege of helping to build a part of it.)

The new EasyEquities UI went live earlier this week, and there’s quite a bit more to it than meets the eye.

Our feed has been a mixture of compliments, questions, bug reports and the occasional person who has decided a new font marks the end of civilisation.

With that said though, I think it is receiving an overwhelmingly positive and well-deserved response.

Sure, these things have a way of making everyone a design critic - but our new UI looks genuinely good.

That said, not every sharp reaction is a worthy design verdict. Althea covered this well in her [Fylom episode on redesign habit shock](https://fylom.com/e/wXT2LkzUjlim): after a major update, week one can look like a disaster even when the interface is better. As she put it, code ships in a day… users’ brains migrate over weeks.

The land of software and UI/UX often gets me thinking...

More than that. Deliberating...

UI, UX, UI, UX - product product product. Terms I obviously hear a lot.

I think I can comfortably (and somewhat controversially) say I absolutely do not agree with how often people say beauty is subjective... least of all when it comes to UI.

I think beauty is predominantly objective. Beauty is structure (or a considered lack of it). Fractals. Pattern. The things we keep finding ourselves drawn to.

Personal taste moves us around at the edges, obviously, but humans reach consensus on what is beautiful far more often than that little phrase allows. [Researchers studying facial attractiveness found surprisingly high agreement across ten different cultures](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0225549), and the wider body of work keeps returning to proportion, symmetry and how fluently our brains can process what we see.

There is structure hiding underneath beauty. Ratios. Balance. Mathematics.

I won’t get into the golden ratio and my geometry journal here. That is a blog post for another time.

My point is simply that the new UI has that quality. The refinements just make sense. The colours hold together. A great many tiny decisions line up, and you can feel the order of it before you understand why.

So I’ve decided to share some thoughts on why I’ve never been more bullish on EasyEquities.

(Bullish on the company, the mission and our ability to execute. This is not a nudge to buy our shares.)

## What actually changed

Visually, yes, this is a rebrand.

There is new colour, new typography, a new illustration system and the whole thing feels much more considered.

EasyEquities, EasyCrypto, EasyProperties, EasyCredit and EasyProtect now feel like parts of the same universe.

That is partly visual. The bit I care about more is I feel we’ve started embracing a very simple philosophy. less is more. It lines up with what I was getting at in [When Peyotes Bloom](/posts/closing-out-my-20s/) when I wrote about simple subtractions (less is very often more).

Someone looking for life insurance should not have to guess what “EasyProtect” does. Someone looking for a home loan should be able to find “Get a home loan” without knowing that EasyMortgages exists.

It sounds painfully obvious when you write it out like that. Sometimes putting the word Easy in front of something doesn’t always make it easier to understand.

So I think that has inadvertently become one of the many wins in the rebrand.

We are remembering who we are: a business pioneering a super investment app, a wealth-building tool for South Africans, and in due course a global audience.

I spend a fair amount of time talking to people who were never really taught about finance.

Those conversations are rarely about indifference. They are usually about trying to make sense of something that was never properly explained... and I think commonly reaching for the nearest comparison that feels true.

And every now and then somebody compares investing to gambling.

I can see how the two get conflated from a distance. If all you notice is a phone screen and a moving number, they can look uncomfortably similar.

Side note: is it just me, or do gambling billboards have a sixth sense for neighbourhoods that are already deteriorating?

That bothers me.

EasyEquities stands for something very different.

Investing, at least to me, is about ownership, patience and compounding. The point is to build wealth over time. There is an entire academic discipline and professional industry behind it (my first academic path was Investment Management, so bear with me as I lightly jump into some of the numbers).

That long-run framing is one of the things my professors at university drilled into me early, and it has stayed.

It is not always a cheerful thing to write about heading into year-end with a little blood in the markets (especially in US tech) and with plenty of portfolios feeling the sting. Mine included.

If you are on that investment journey and the recent volatility has hurt, I hope it helps a little to know the feeling is shared. My own investing happens through EasyEquities itself, which makes the whole thing oddly intimate. I help build the app, and my life’s savings live inside it as a customer.

The lesson was never that markets only go up. It was that time is the quiet variable that does most of the work, and that compounding only starts to look like magic on the scale of decades.

That is also how I tend to think about EasyEquities and its people: as pillars of an institution that should still be standing in South Africa, and beyond, long after any single year of market weather. We have very good people. That showed up again recently in our staff awards, and I am glad we take the time to acknowledge hard work properly.

There is almost something sacred about building in fintech with that horizon in mind. People are here on the scale of decades, because that is what it takes to operate a system with a responsibility this great. We sometimes joke about being “lifers” in the office... and for a product built on compounding, half the joke is that it is not really a joke.

Part of our job is making investing accessible and then helping people understand what they are participating in (and why time matters).

The app now feels more cohesive while remaining familiar.

…and I think that is particularly important (speaking from experience).

We’ve reskinned existing journeys. We haven’t suddenly created a collection of new ones. A lot of good UI/UX thinking was already in place - set down by teams who were hard at work long before Telescope arrived - and this launch built on that foundation rather than starting from scratch.

Our Telescope team’s philosophy behind the work is simple and powerful: clear in what it shows, warm in how it feels and unmistakably Easy.

I only recently discovered that Althea, Telescope’s Head of Product, is also an artist. Her work completely blew me away when I got curious enough to take a gander at [her Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/altheaschutte/).

In 2026, raw coding ability on its own no longer impresses me the way it once did. Code is becoming abundant.

Althea’s art stopped me in my tracks. You can feel years of seeing and practising sitting behind it.

I feel it’s safe to assume that part of her life touched this launch. I just love knowing that this kind of eye exists inside the team. It speaks to the range of people now working together across product, engineering and design. Beyond working life, it has sparked a desire to have more art in my life altogether.

That thinking reached twelve websites, a rebuilt support portal and every app in one coordinated launch. Teams across South Africa, Australia and the Philippines worked through the night to bring it over the line.

The teams have already polished the majority of the rough edges in a matter of hours. Unprecedented, and I continue to be impressed.

Some of what comes next will be real niggles. Some of it will be that migration still underway. Our job is to listen closely (especially to the data), fix what is broken, and keep empowering the sensitive act of investing.

What excites me is that we’re unlocking an absolutely insane cadence in an environment of high complexity (it’s really not as easy as it looks from the outside).

A roadmap that used to look like three years can suddenly start looking like a few months.

## Looking through Telescope

Earlier this year, Charles and the Group made the decision to acquire Telescope AI.

I suspect we’ll look back on it as pivotal.

Technically, yes, we procured a technology stack.

But when one thinks about what we actually bought, it helps to think about the people first.

A team of Australian pioneers with a serious drive to succeed.

I’ve always said that where there’s a will, there’s a way. And this team has a very strong will.

There was obviously a little bit of rapport that was built between us and Telescope before the acquisition, but Charles saw something well beyond the tech stack that others didn’t - a team of people that can figure sh!t out, with a fresh pair of eyes for good product.

As [Charles wrote when the deal was announced](https://blogs.easyequities.co.za/the-savage-take-making-investment-intelligence-available-to-everyone), it was a conviction tested through product.

The acquisition brought Telescope’s technology into the Group and Luc Pettett into the role of Chief AI Officer.

They also arrived without the assumptions that naturally collect inside a company over time.

You can feel complexity collecting inside a company as it grows. There is always one more meeting, usually because a handover went wrong once and nobody remembers who is allowed to kill it.

I had spoken about that same problem earlier in the year, when one of my former professors invited me back to the University of Pretoria to give [a guest lecture on Agile work](https://youtu.be/8TjvGYNMpTY) to his Honours class.

It was a privilege (and slightly surreal) to return as a guest to a room I had once known as a student.

Complexity grows much faster than the headcount suggests (this was essentially the point of my lecture).

Over time, good ideas can end up travelling through so many meetings, handovers and permission gates that the original problem has changed before the solution arrives.

We look after people’s money, so controls are essential. The difficult bit is noticing when a control stopped protecting a client and simply became part of the furniture.

This launch brought together Telescope’s design discipline, our teams’ knowledge and AI-assisted building and testing. It required many capable people to move in the same direction without every decision travelling through the same narrow doorway.

We’re still working through plenty of organisational complexity (obviously).

Still, this launch proved that we can dismantle some of our own unnecessary bottlenecks - and that’s an achievement that I’m truly admiring - to a certain extent - and there is no easier way to put it - my flabbers are gasted.

## Three years

That old Investment Management part of my brain still wants a scoreboard behind the word “bullish”.

One comparison in Charles’ FY2026 letter says enough.

Three years ago, Easy Group spent R241 million to earn R238 million and lost R44 million.

This year, it spent R328 million to earn R516 million and made R161 million.

At Group level, headline earnings per share went from a loss of 2.05 cents in FY2023 to earnings of 5.47 cents this year.

As Charles put it, we did not cut our way there. We served a great many more people for not many more rand.

This is of course my grossly oversimplified takeaway.

## The next twelve months

Today is mostly the skin. The journeys underneath are familiar (which was the point).

The bit I’m itching for is the next twelve months.

Better journeys. Fewer dead ends between products. Less of that feeling that you have somehow wandered into a different Easy.

It has also been a long year.

A great many people worked hard, and ultimately we came together as a team and delivered value.

Working for EasyEquities has been an incredible privilege thus far.

I’m grateful to be part of a team that challenges me honestly. I don’t always enjoy that in the moment (who does), but I’ve learned an enormous amount from it.

Five years ago I joined a very different business. The numbers above say as much. Watching teams across three countries pull off this launch tells me we can keep changing as we grow.

Now we have to turn that pace into better experiences for the people who trust us with their money.

That is why I’m bullish.

For now, though, I’m starting to focus my sights on spending time with Ashton and Micky, friends, family and resting well over the December holidays. Kicking it in KZN, Southbroom. Yass.

So that we can come back to 2027 with a bang.

Five years in, and I must say, with the greatest level of sincerity, I feel like this is just the beginning for EasyEquities.
